Executive Summary (Direct Answer)

Principal AI buyers price corporate archives primarily on cross-system linkage, employee count, years of history, and domain scarcity—not annual revenue. Published 2026 schedules show US companies with 20+ employees running daily workflows in Gmail, Google Chat, and Google Drive qualify for up to $500,000 on Mercor’s Google Workspace Power User track, single-system codebases price between $20,000 and $500,000, multi-system enterprise archives at micro1 price in $100K–$500K and $500K–$3M+ tiers, and industrial/manufacturing shop-floor records reach up to $1.5M.

Why Cross-System Linkage Multiplies Payouts Over Isolated Silos

An isolated CSV export of closed CRM deals tells an AI model what happened, but not how a human operator achieved it. When a buyer can trace a customer escalation from an inbound Zendesk ticket into an internal Slack engineering triage thread, a GitHub pull request fix, and a Salesforce renewal note, that connected trajectory forms a complete training environment for enterprise AI agents.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Google Workspace have its own dedicated $500K buyer track?
Frontier labs train computer-use and workspace agents to draft spreadsheets, presentations, and cross-document analyses. US companies with 20+ employees collaborating daily across Google Drive, Sheets, Slides, Gmail, and Chat hold native collaborative artifacts that synthetic generators cannot mimic.
Does low company revenue reduce what our operational data is worth?
No. AI buyers price the volume, cleanliness, years of history, and workflow complexity of your records—not your current EBITDA or revenue growth.
