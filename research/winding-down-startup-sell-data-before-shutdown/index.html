Who Has Legal Authority to Sign for a Closing or Closed Company?

  • Solvent Wind-Down (Pre-Dissolution or Statutory Wind-Up): Under Delaware General Corporation Law § 278 (and parallel LLC statutes), a dissolved entity continues for the purpose of winding up and disposing of assets. The Board of Directors (with required preferred-shareholder or secured-lender consents) signs and receives wire settlement into the wind-down bank account.
  • Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors (ABC) or Bankruptcy: Once assigned or filed, founders no longer sign personally—the Assignee or Chapter 7/11 Trustee holds statutory authority to monetize estate assets, and customer PII restricted by legacy privacy policies must be excluded upfront (11 U.S.C. § 332).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is an upfront lump-sum license better than revenue-share for a shutting-down startup?
A closing startup operates on a fixed accounting and tax dissolution deadline. Programs like micro1 pay 100% within 30 days of acceptance so proceeds can be distributed once to creditors and shareholders, whereas open-ended revenue-share agreements require keeping a legal entity and bank account alive indefinitely.
Can we monetize our startup's data if we only had 25–40 employees at our peak?
Yes. Buyers evaluate peak historical headcount reflected in the archive, not how many employees remain on payroll during the final week of shutdown. micro1's defunct track evaluates 25–30+ peak FTE archives, and Mercor's code and AI-product tracks start at 5–15+ engineers.
